## OCR Domain Hint: High School Physics (高校物理)

When recognizing handwritten physics expressions at the high school level:

### Symbol recognition priorities
- v₀ (subscript 0) → initial velocity; distinguish from v (current velocity)
- ω (omega, lowercase) → angular velocity or angular frequency \omega
- Ω (omega, uppercase) → resistance unit ohm
- μ → friction coefficient \mu (not micro prefix in mechanics context)
- θ → angle \theta; often used for angle of incline or phase
- ε₀ → permittivity of free space \varepsilon_0
- Φ (uppercase phi) → magnetic flux \Phi
- Σ (uppercase sigma) → summation OR surface charge density — context determines
- Δ before a quantity → change in that quantity (Δv = v - v₀)
- Single-dot overhead (ẋ) → dx/dt; double-dot (ẍ) → d²x/dt²
- Vector arrow overhead → vector quantity; render as \vec{F}, \vec{v} etc.

### Common expression patterns
- v = v₀ + at → kinematic equation
- x = v₀t + ½at² → position as function of time
- F = GMm/r² → gravitational force
- n₁sinθ₁ = n₂sinθ₂ → Snell's law (refraction)
- 1/f = 1/a + 1/b → thin lens equation
- emf = -dΦ/dt → Faraday's law of induction
- E = hf → photon energy (h is Planck constant)

### Handwriting quirks to watch for
- "g" (gravitational acceleration 9.8 m/s²) vs "g" (gram unit): unit if after number
- Subscripts i, o on optics quantities → image/object distance (dᵢ, d_o)
- Superscript 2 on velocity (v²) → square, not second derivative
- Large parentheses around fractions → grouping, not absolute value
- Over-bar on a quantity → sometimes used for average (v̄ = average velocity)
