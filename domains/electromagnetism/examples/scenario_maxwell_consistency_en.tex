Consider the electromagnetic field. Prepare the electric field $\mathbf E$ and magnetic field $\mathbf B$ as vector fields (components registered abstractly, to be assigned concrete expressions next).

E as a vector field

\[
  E
\]

B as a vector field

\[
  B
\]

Assign the components of the electric field for a linearly polarized plane wave traveling in the $+x$ direction (amplitude 1, wavevector $k$, angular frequency $\omega$).

$E_x = 0$

\begin{equation}
  E_x = 0
  \label{eq:E1}
\end{equation}

$E_y = cos(k x - w t)$

\begin{equation}
  E_y = \cos(k x - w t)
  \label{eq:E2}
\end{equation}

$E_z = 0$

\begin{equation}
  E_z = 0
  \label{eq:E3}
\end{equation}

Assign the corresponding components of the magnetic field.

$B_x = 0$

\begin{equation}
  B_x = 0
  \label{eq:E4}
\end{equation}

$B_y = 0$

\begin{equation}
  B_y = 0
  \label{eq:E5}
\end{equation}

$B_z = cos(k x - w t)/c$

\begin{equation}
  B_z = \cos(k x - w t)/c
  \label{eq:E6}
\end{equation}

Compute the divergence of the electric field $\nabla\cdot\mathbf E$. In vacuum (no charge), this should vanish identically (Gauss's law).

$\nabla \cdot E$

\begin{equation}
  0
  \label{eq:E7}
\end{equation}

Compute the divergence of the magnetic field $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B$. Since no magnetic charge exists, this should vanish identically.

$\nabla \cdot B$

\begin{equation}
  0
  \label{eq:E8}
\end{equation}

Compute the curl of the electric field $\nabla\times\mathbf E$ (the left-hand side of Faraday's law).

$\nabla \times E$

\begin{equation}
  \left[ 0 , 0 , -\left(k \sin \left(k x-t w\right)\right) \right]
  \label{eq:E9}
\end{equation}

Compute the curl of the magnetic field $\nabla\times\mathbf B$ (the left-hand side of Ampere's law).

$\nabla \times B$

\begin{equation}
  \left[ 0 , \frac{k \sin \left(k x-t w\right)}{c} , 0 \right]
  \label{eq:E10}
\end{equation}

Since $\partial/\partial t$ cannot act directly on a vector field, take the time derivative component by component. Here, take the partial derivative of the nonzero component $B_z$ with respect to $t$.

Take the partial derivative of $B_z$ with respect to t

\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial}{\partial t} B_z(x,y,z) = \frac{w \sin \left(k x-t w\right)}{c}
  \label{eq:E11}
\end{equation}

Assemble the derivative just found into a new vector field, which we name `dBdt` — a plain identifier chosen here to stand for $d\mathbf B/dt$ (it is not a CAS keyword or special syntax; any other name would work identically), assigning each component in turn.

dBdt as a vector field

\[
  dBdt
\]

$dBdt_x = 0$

\begin{equation}
  dBdt_x = 0
  \label{eq:E12}
\end{equation}

$dBdt_y = 0$

\begin{equation}
  dBdt_y = 0
  \label{eq:E13}
\end{equation}

$dBdt_z = w sin(k x - w t)/c$

\begin{equation}
  dBdt_z = w \sin(k x - w t)/c
  \label{eq:E14}
\end{equation}

Confirm that the assembled $d\mathbf B/dt$ can be used in further calculations (such as a divergence) by computing its divergence.

$\nabla \cdot dBdt$

\begin{equation}
  0
  \label{eq:E15}
\end{equation}

Register the $z$-components of Faraday's law $\nabla\times\mathbf E=-\partial\mathbf B/\partial t$ as an equality (comparing the $z$-component of each side).

$-k sin(k x - w t) = -w sin(k x - w t)/c$

\begin{equation}
  -k \sin(k x - w t) = -w \sin(k x - w t)/c
  \label{eq:E16}
\end{equation}

Solve this equality for the angular frequency $\omega$ to obtain the consistency condition (dispersion relation) $\omega=ck$.

Solve E16 for w

\begin{equation}
  w = \frac{k x}{t}
  \label{eq:E17}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
  w = c k
  \label{eq:E18}
\end{equation}
