Perturbation theory for a two-level system. $H=H_0+\lambda V$, with $H_0$ eigenvalues $E_a, E_b$ and a purely off-diagonal $V$; $\lambda$ is the coupling.

Define Ea, Eb, V, $\lambda$ as scalars

\[
  Ea, Eb, V, \lambda
\]

Register the Hamiltonian as a $2\times2$ matrix.

$H = [Ea, \lambda V; \lambda V, Eb]$

\begin{equation}
  H = \begin{pmatrix} Ea & \lambda V \\ \lambda V & Eb \end{pmatrix}
  \label{eq:E1}
\end{equation}

For a $2\times2$ the eigenvalues are exact, and the perturbation series is just that closed form expanded in $\lambda$ — no dedicated "perturbation expansion" operation is needed.

eigenvalues of E1

\begin{equation}
  \left\{ -\left(\frac{\sqrt{4 V^2 \lambda^2+Eb^2-2 Ea Eb+Ea^2}-Eb-Ea}{2}\right) , \frac{\sqrt{4 V^2 \lambda^2+Eb^2-2 Ea Eb+Ea^2}+Eb+Ea}{2} \right\}
  \label{eq:E2}
\end{equation}

Register the lower branch.

$(Ea + Eb - \sqrt{4 V^2 \lambda^2 + (Ea-Eb)^2})/2$

\begin{equation}
  (Ea + Eb - \sqrt{4 V^2 \lambda^2 + (Ea-Eb)^2})/2
  \label{eq:E3}
\end{equation}

Expand to first order. The perturbation is off-diagonal ($\langle n|V|n\rangle=0$), so the first-order correction should vanish.

taylor expand E3 in $\lambda$ around 0 to order 1

\begin{equation}
  Ea
  \label{eq:E4}
\end{equation}

Expand to second order. This should give the textbook $E^{(2)}_n=|\langle m|V|n\rangle|^2/(E_n-E_m)$.

taylor expand E3 in $\lambda$ around 0 to order 2

\begin{equation}
  Ea-\frac{V^2 \lambda^2}{Eb-Ea}
  \label{eq:E5}
\end{equation}

Do the same for the upper branch. The sign is opposite — the two levels repel.

$(Ea + Eb + \sqrt{4 V^2 \lambda^2 + (Ea-Eb)^2})/2$

\begin{equation}
  (Ea + Eb + \sqrt{4 V^2 \lambda^2 + (Ea-Eb)^2})/2
  \label{eq:E6}
\end{equation}

taylor expand E6 in $\lambda$ around 0 to order 2

\begin{equation}
  \frac{V^2 \lambda^2}{Eb-Ea}+Eb
  \label{eq:E7}
\end{equation}

The check. The sum of the two levels is the trace $E_a+E_b$, and that holds exactly, so the second-order contributions must cancel if the series is right.

$E5 + E7$

\begin{equation}
  E5 + E7
  \label{eq:E8}
\end{equation}

simplify E8

\begin{equation}
  Eb+Ea
  \label{eq:E9}
\end{equation}
