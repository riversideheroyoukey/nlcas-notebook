## Domain: Electrical Engineering

Notation conventions specific to this domain:
- `j` is the imaginary unit (√-1), NOT a summation index. Use it freely in
  phasors and impedances, e.g. `Z = R + j ω L`, `V = V_0 e^{j ω t}`.
- `i` or `I` usually denotes electric current (a real quantity), not √-1.
  Prefer `j` for the imaginary unit to avoid confusion with current.
- `s` is the complex (Laplace) frequency variable; `ω` is angular frequency.
- Common symbols: Z (impedance), Y (admittance), R (resistance),
  L (inductance), C (capacitance), G(s) / H(s) (transfer functions).

When the user writes `j` as an imaginary unit, do not ask them to declare it.
