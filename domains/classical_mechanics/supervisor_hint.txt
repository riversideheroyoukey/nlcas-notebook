## Domain: Classical (Newtonian) Mechanics

### Model: q̇ = dq/dt — velocity is the time-derivative of position

Unlike analytical mechanics, this domain treats q as a function of time q(t) and
its velocity q̇ as the genuine time-derivative dq/dt (DEPENDENT on q).  Use this
domain for Newtonian problems: equations of motion integrated directly,
trajectories, collisions, projectile motion, orbits, conservation laws checked
by direct time-differentiation.

**Declare moving quantities as time-dependent**, e.g.
"x を時間依存関数として定義する".  Then `\dot{x}` means dx/dt and d/dt of any
expression follows the ordinary chain rule through x(t).

Common symbols and their roles:
- x, y, r, q: positions — functions of t
- ẋ (input `\dot{x}`): velocity = dx/dt
- ẍ (input `\ddot{x}`): acceleration = d²x/dt²
- v: velocity;  a: acceleration;  p = m v: momentum
- F: force;  m: mass;  g: gravitational acceleration
- T: kinetic energy;  V/U: potential energy;  E: total energy

Input velocities with the math notation `\dot{x}`.  Never ask the user to type
CAS syntax such as `diff(x,t)` — that violates Core Principle #1.

When the user writes "x を t で微分", interpret as the ordinary time-derivative
d/dt (chain rule through x(t)), NOT a Lagrangian total derivative over
independent atoms.

This domain deliberately does NOT enable the analytical-mechanics independent
velocity model.  Choose `analytical_mechanics` instead when working with
Lagrangians/Hamiltonians and Euler-Lagrange equations.
See docs/19_analytical_mechanics_independent_variables.md for the distinction.
