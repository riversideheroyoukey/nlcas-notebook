## OCR Domain Hint: High School Mathematics (高校数学)

When recognizing handwritten mathematical expressions at the high school level:

### Symbol recognition priorities
- d/dx or dy/dx → derivative notation \frac{d}{dx} or \frac{dy}{dx}
- ∫ with limits → definite integral \int_{a}^{b}; without limits → indefinite \int
- Σ with limits → summation \sum_{k=1}^{n}
- lim with subscript → limit \lim_{x \to a}
- log without base → could be log₁₀ or ln depending on context; prefer \log
- ln → natural logarithm \ln
- e (standalone, upright) → Napier's constant \mathrm{e} or e
- vec arrow over letter → vector \vec{a}
- |v| with vector → magnitude \|\vec{v}\| or |v|
- nCr or C(n,r) with stacked notation → \binom{n}{r}
- i (standalone in complex context) → imaginary unit i (i² = -1)
- Re(...), Im(...) → real/imaginary parts

### Common expression patterns
- f'(x), f''(x) → first/second derivative (prime notation)
- [F(x)]_a^b → definite integral evaluation \left[F(x)\right]_a^b
- a·b (dot between vectors) → dot product \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}
- Matrix enclosed in parentheses or brackets → 2×2 or 3×3 matrix
- a_n with subscript n → n-th term of sequence

### Handwriting quirks to watch for
- Prime mark (') on function → derivative, not "feet" or "minutes"
- Σ vs S (capital): Σ has sharp angles, S is curved
- ∫ vs long S: context determines; in calculus context assume integral
- Superscript −1 on function → inverse function f⁻¹, not reciprocal
- θ (theta) in trigonometry context → \theta
- φ or ϕ → \phi or \varphi; clarify from context
