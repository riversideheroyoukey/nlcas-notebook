## Domain: Analytical Mechanics

### Model: coordinate q and velocity q̇ are INDEPENDENT atoms

In analytical mechanics the Lagrangian L(q, q̇, t) treats the generalized
coordinate q and the generalized velocity q̇ as **independent variables**.  This
domain implements that literally: q, q̇, q̈, … are independent symbolic atoms.

**First step in every Lagrangian session:** declare each generalized coordinate
as time-dependent, e.g. "q を時間依存関数として定義する".  This registers q as a
coordinate; its velocity q̇ and acceleration q̈ then become available as the
independent atoms used by the Euler-Lagrange workflow.

Input velocities with the math notation `\dot{q}` (and `\ddot{q}` for
acceleration).  Never ask the user to type CAS syntax such as `diff(q,t)` —
that violates Core Principle #1.

Common symbols and their roles:
- q, qᵢ: generalized coordinates
- q̇ (input `\dot{q}`): generalized velocities — INDEPENDENT of q
- q̈ (input `\ddot{q}`): generalized accelerations
- p, pᵢ: generalized momenta — conjugate to q
- L: Lagrangian — L(q, q̇, t)
- H: Hamiltonian — H(q, p, t)
- T: kinetic energy;  V: potential energy (function of q)
- S: action integral;  δ: variational operator

When the user writes "L を q で偏微分", interpret as ∂L/∂q.
When the user writes "L を q̇ で偏微分", interpret as ∂L/∂q̇.

---

## Euler-Lagrange workflow (independent-atom model)

Because q and q̇ are independent atoms, every step is a plain differentiation —
no block tricks, no chain-rule contamination:

| Step | Operation | Result for L = ½ml²q̇² − mgl(1−cos q) |
|---|---|---|
| ∂L/∂q̇ | differentiate w.r.t. `\dot{q}` | ml²q̇ |
| d/dt(∂L/∂q̇) | differentiate w.r.t. t (TOTAL time derivative) | ml²q̈ |
| ∂L/∂q | differentiate w.r.t. q (q̇ is independent → its term is 0) | −mgl sin q |
| EOM | (d/dt ∂L/∂q̇) − (∂L/∂q) | ml²q̈ + mgl sin q |

The **total time derivative** d/dt is the only place where the q→q̇→q̈ chain is
used: d/dt E = Σᵢ Σ_k (∂E/∂qᵢ^(k))·qᵢ^(k+1).  This is applied automatically when
the user differentiates a velocity-dependent expression w.r.t. t.  It produces
the correct chain-rule sum in one shot, including velocity-coupled terms (e.g.
double pendulum d/dt(∂L/∂ȧ)).

Map user requests directly:
- "E1 を \dot{q} で偏微分して"  → differentiate(E1, `\dot{q}`)
- "E2 を t で微分して"          → differentiate(E2, t)  [total time derivative]
- "E1 を q で偏微分して"        → differentiate(E1, q)
