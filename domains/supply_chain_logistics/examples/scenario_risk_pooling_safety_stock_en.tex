Define the general safety-stock formula $SS=z\sigma\sqrt{L}$ ($z$: service-level factor, $\sigma$: demand standard deviation, $L$: lead time).

$SS = z \sigma \sqrt{L}$

\begin{equation}
  SS = z \sigma \sqrt{L}
  \label{eq:E1}
\end{equation}

Consider pooling the demand of $n$ locations into a single central warehouse. Under the i.i.d. assumption, variances add, so the effective lead-time term becomes $nL$ instead of $L$. Substitute this replacement into the original expression (the original expression itself remains available as the single-location case).

In E1, substitute n * L for L

\begin{equation}
  SS = \sqrt{L n} \sigma z
  \label{eq:E2}
\end{equation}

Take the ratio of the pooled safety stock to the total safety stock of the $n$ separate locations ($n$ times the original expression), to confirm the risk-pooling effect (the square-root law $1/\sqrt n$).

$ratio = E2 / (n E1)$

\begin{equation}
  ratio = \frac{\sqrt{L n}}{\sqrt{L} n}
  \label{eq:E3}
\end{equation}

$E3=\sqrt{Ln}/(\sqrt{L}\,n)$ is algebraically $1/\sqrt{n}$, but Maxima's general `simplify` does not combine the two separate radicals on its own. Apply the dedicated radical-simplification operation to reduce it to the familiar closed form.

$radical_simplify$ E3

\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
  \label{eq:E4}
\end{equation}
