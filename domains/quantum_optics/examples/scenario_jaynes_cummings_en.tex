Consider the Jaynes-Cummings model (a photon interacting with a two-level molecule). Prepare the photon frequency $\omega$, the molecular transition frequency $\omega_0$, and the light-matter coupling constant $g$ as real parameters.

Define omega as a real parameter

\[
  \omega
\]

Define $\omega_0$ as a real parameter

\[
  \omega_{0}
\]

Define g as a real parameter

\[
  g
\]

Prepare the photon annihilation/creation operators $a,a^\dagger$ as boson operators, and the molecule's two-level (spin-1/2) operator $S$ as a spin operator.

a as the creation operator for photon

\[
  a
\]

S as the spin operator

\[
  S, S^{x}, S^{y}, S^{z}
\]

Register the Jaynes-Cummings Hamiltonian $H=\omega a^\dagger a+\omega_0S_z+g(aS^++a^\dagger S^-)$, entered with $S^\pm=S_x\pm iS_y$ expanded out. The first term is the photon energy, the second the molecular excitation energy, and the third the light-matter interaction from photon absorption/emission.

$\omega a'(1) a(1) + \omega_0 Sz(1) + g (a(1) (Sx(1) + i Sy(1)) + a'(1) (Sx(1) - i Sy(1)))$

\begin{equation}
  \omega a^{\dagger}(1) a(1) + \omega_0 Sz(1) + g (a(1) (Sx(1) + i Sy(1)) + a^{\dagger}(1) (Sx(1) - i Sy(1)))
  \label{eq:E1}
\end{equation}

Compute the Heisenberg equation of motion for the photon operator $a$.

Heisenberg equation of E1 for a(1)

\begin{equation}
  \frac{d a(1)}{d t} = - g \mathit{i} Sx_1 - g Sy_1 - \omega \mathit{i} {a}_{1}
  \label{eq:E2}
\end{equation}

Compute the Heisenberg equation of motion for the molecular operator $S_x$.

Heisenberg equation of E1 for Sx(1)

\begin{equation}
  \frac{d Sx(1)}{d t} = - 2 \omega_{0} Sy_1 - 2 g \mathit{i} {a}_{1}^{\dagger} Sz_1 + 2 g \mathit{i} Sz_1 {a}_{1}
  \label{eq:E3}
\end{equation}

Define the excitation-number operator $N_{\rm ex}=a^\dagger a+S_z/2$ (the sum of the photon number and the molecule's spin $z$-component).

a'(1)*a(1) + Sz(1)/2

\begin{equation}
  a^{\dagger}(1) a(1) + Sz(1)/2
  \label{eq:E4}
\end{equation}

Compute the commutator of the Hamiltonian $H$ and the excitation-number operator $N_{\rm ex}$. Physically, each of $H$'s three terms individually conserves the total excitation number: the photon-energy and molecule-energy terms are diagonal in the excitation-number basis, and the interaction term $g(aS^++a^\dagger S^-)$ only ever converts one photon into one molecular excitation or vice versa (photon absorption/emission), never changing $a^\dagger a+S_z$'s total. This is why the CAS-computed commutator collapses to exactly 0 rather than some residual expression — a genuinely computed cancellation, not a fixed answer. (The CAS applies operator identities and returns the final simplified value directly; it does not expose the intermediate term-by-term expansion as a separate visible step.)

[E1, E4]

\begin{equation}
  0
  \label{eq:E5}
\end{equation}
