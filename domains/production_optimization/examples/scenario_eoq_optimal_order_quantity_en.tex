Given annual demand $D$, ordering cost $K$ per order, holding cost $h$ per unit, and order quantity $Q$, define the total cost $C(Q)=KD/Q+hQ/2$ (the sum of the ordering cost, "number of orders $D/Q$ times $K$", and the holding cost, "average inventory $Q/2$ times $h$").

$C = K D / Q + h Q / 2$

\begin{equation}
  C = K D / Q + h Q / 2
  \label{eq:E1}
\end{equation}

To find the order quantity that minimizes the total cost, take the total derivative of $C$ with respect to $Q$.

Take the total derivative of E1 with respect to Q

\begin{equation}
  \frac{dC}{dQ} = \frac{h}{2}-\frac{D K}{Q^2}
  \label{eq:E2}
\end{equation}

Set the derivative to zero and solve for $Q$ to find the candidate order quantities that minimize the cost.

Solve E2 for Q

\begin{equation}
  Q = -\sqrt{2} \sqrt{\frac{D K}{h}}
  \label{eq:E3}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
  Q = \sqrt{2} \sqrt{\frac{D K}{h}}
  \label{eq:E4}
\end{equation}

Substitute the economically meaningful positive order quantity $Q^*$ from the solutions just found into the total-cost expression $C(Q)$ to obtain the minimum total cost.

Substitute E4 into E1

\begin{equation}
  C = {{\sqrt{2} D K}\over{\sqrt{\frac{D K}{h}}}}
  \label{eq:E5}
\end{equation}
